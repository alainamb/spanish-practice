Vocabulary challenge: How many -erías can you find on a street in Mexico City?

Introduction

The suffix -ería turns a noun or a verb into the name of a shop that sells the related goods or services.

For example:

  • dulces (sweets/candy) → dulcería (candy shop)
  • tacos → taquería (with a spelling change from "c" to "qu" to keep the hard "c" sound)

And my favorite:

  • verduras (veggies) → verdulería (the second "r" in verduras becomes an "l" to avoid ending the word in "-rería," though not every -ería avoids that pattern)

To help you learn this pattern, we invite you to take a virtual stroll, drive, bike ride, whatever you prefer to call it, down Avenida Centenario, identifying -erías and other places as you go.

Avenida Centenario, in Mexico City, is a street with a lot of -erías. Some time back, we found 23 of them on this street.

Instructions

Starting from the Google Street View embedded below, go for a virtual "walk/bike ride/drive" on Avenida Centenario. Keep a list of the -erías and the other places you find there.

As you go, also look for the common city places in the glossary underneath the map, like clínica, restaurante, or tienda. Feel free to take the identification of places and objects in Spanish as far as you like.

When you're done, compare your results with another group's work, or with the -erías collection we made for this street. Walking down this avenue, we found twenty-three -erías in one day, without exhausting the possibilities:

Additional Activity: More -Erías from Our Network

Friends and colleagues in our network kindly shared these lists of -erías with us. Look them over and see if you can figure out what each shop sells, then keep an eye out for them on Avenida Centenario or on the streets where you live.

  • pulquería, tintorería
  • tlapalería, ferretería, bonetería
  • panadería, licorería, frutería, librería, revistería, peletería, mercería
  • lonchería, tintorería, bonetería, pulpería
  • birrería (or birriería), cervecería
  • jarcería, papelería, herrería, mueblería, paletería, heladería, peletería
  • cancelería, lonchería, plomería, pozolería, recaudería, refaccionaria, vidriería

A fun fact: Tintorería (dry cleaner's) comes from tinta or tinte (dye). Etymologically, it refers to dyeing clothes to hide stains rather than cleaning or removing them.

We're looking forward to hearing about any -erías you spot that we haven't thought of yet!
